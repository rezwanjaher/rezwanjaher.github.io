About Me

I am an MS student in Applied Mathematics and Computational Science at North South University (NSU), Dhaka, where I work as a Graduate Research Assistant at the Center for Applied Scientific Computing (CASC) and as a Graduate Teaching Assistant. I hold a BSc in Industrial and Production Engineering from Shahjalal University of Science and Technology (SUST). Before returning to research, I spent six years in industry as an industrial engineer, working on simulation, inventory control, and process improvement.

My research is on decision-making under uncertainty with limited budgets, applied to public-health and ecological control. I study when and where to spend an intervention budget, using linear programming and heuristics, partially observable Markov decision processes and deep reinforcement learning, and distributionally robust optimization. I care about fair comparisons: every learned policy is tested against strong, well-built baselines.

Efficacy Regimes in Vector-Borne Epidemic Control: Timing, Targeting, and the Price of Information (Submitted to an INFORMS journal)

Pre-print SSRN 7705658 GitHub Code Zenodo Archive

Md Rezwan Jaher, Md. Sumon Hossain, Md Ahsanul Haque
November 2026

Summary

  • Studied spatio-temporal budget allocation for epidemic control with fast-decaying interventions and costly surveillance, calibrated to dengue in Dhaka North (54 wards).
  • Found two efficacy thresholds that split vector control into three regimes: spread the budget, delay and then concentrate, or concentrate at once.
  • Proved that no schedule of habitat control makes vector extinction stable, while sustained adult killing eliminates the vector above a coverage threshold.
  • Trained a deep reinforcement-learning (POMDP) controller and compared it with linear-programming and greedy allocation policies; code, data, and trained agents are public.

When to Start Spraying: Distributionally Robust Vector Control under Uncertain Field Efficacy (Working paper, in preparation)

Md Rezwan Jaher, Md. Sumon Hossain, Md. Mamun Molla

Summary

  • Extends the dengue study to the case where field spray efficacy is unknown, using Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization with regret objectives.
  • Shows that the robust start rule is the plug-in rule plus a computable margin, and that epidemic growth speed decides when efficacy uncertainty matters.
  • Uses street-canyon CFD simulations of spray dispersion (OpenFOAM) to estimate field efficacy for a Dhaka case study.

Deconstructing Intraocular Pressure: A Non-invasive Multi-Stage Probabilistic Inverse Framework (Completed two review rounds at INFORMS Journal on Computing, including a major revision)

Pre-print ArXiv 2509.14167 GitHub Code

Md Rezwan Jaher, Abul Mukid Mohammad Mukaddes, A. B. M. Abdul Malek
September 17, 2025

Abstract

Many critical decision-making processes in healthcare and engineering are challenged by the inability to measure key underlying parameters. These scenarios often formulate as mechanistically hierarchical inverse problems, where the observable macroscopic output is multiplicatively entangled with intrinsic micro-scale physics and unmeasurable spatial geometry. Solving such severely underdetermined problems via standard Bayesian inference or simulation-based calibration requires millions of forward evaluations, rendering patient-specific inference computationally intractable at the clinical point-of-care. Conversely, standard black-box empirical models fail to resolve the structural non-identifiability, indiscriminately absorbing structural variance and collapsing to the population mean. To address these coupled clinical and computational challenges, we propose a novel two-stage architectural decomposition. Instead of a monolithic mapping, we functionally separate the inverse problem according to its governing physics. The first stage acts as a constrained inverse surrogate, isolating the intrinsic biophysical parameter by learning the inverse mapping over an idealized reference manifold. The second stage performs empirical structural calibration, learning an effective geometric factor to bridge the idealized physics with in vivo macroscopic reality. To train this architecture under extreme data scarcity, we introduce Physics-Calibrated Data Scaling (PCDS), a highly scalable data generation strategy that dynamically corrects the systematic bias between low-fidelity analytical surrogates and high-fidelity finite element simulations. We demonstrate this framework on the clinical diagnosis of glaucoma, successfully deconstructing a single, routine intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement into its latent hydrodynamic root causes (tissue permeability and outflow facility). By propagating population-level epistemic uncertainty through our trained operators via Monte Carlo sampling, the framework generates a continuous, empirical push-forward distribution of the patient’s physiological state. Our results confirm that while standard data-driven baselines achieve only 37% classification accuracy, our hierarchical decomposition achieves 92.5% diagnostic accuracy, offering a generalizable blueprint for solving ill-posed, data-scarce inverse problems across the physical and biological sciences

Methodological Contributions

  • Developed a novel, physics guided decomposition architecture to make the underdetermined problem tractable by separating biophysical and patient-specific components.
  • Engineered a new hybrid data generation strategy, Physics-Calibrated Data Scaling (PCDS), which generates a physically consistent training dataset (n=120,000) in under 8 hours on a consumer-grade CPU by calibrating a 2D reference model.
  • Enabled robust inference from sparse clinical inputs (age, IOP) using a constrained Monte Carlo simulation to propagate population-level uncertainty and generate empirical distributions of the biomarkers.

Clinical & Scientific Impact

  • Produced model-based estimates of patient-specific trabecular meshwork permeability (KTM), a biomarker of glaucoma that cannot be measured in vivo.
  • Built a computational tool that estimates outflow facility, the clinical gold standard, from routine data alone (age and IOP), and compared its estimates with published clinical measurements.
  • Decomposed a single clinical IOP measurement into a probabilistic hydrodynamic profile, a step toward mechanistic assessment of glaucoma.

This first-author manuscript completed two review rounds at the INFORMS Journal on Computing, including a major revision.

Learning Scalable Policies for Invasive Species Management: Deep Reinforcement Learning for Spatio-Temporal POMDPs (Independent project, Aug.–Dec. 2025; technical report under revision)

Md Rezwan Jaher

Summary

  • Built a POMDP simulator that extends a multistage stochastic MIP model of emerald ash borer control (Kıbiş et al., 2021) with imperfect surveys and stochastic tree-health progression.
  • Trained a graph neural network agent with PPO. On a 10-year, 64-site instance beyond the reach of the MIP, it matched the best survey-and-treat rule and outperformed staged-removal, monitoring, and random-treatment baselines.
  • Re-audited the evaluation in 2026 with the protocol developed in the dengue study and corrected budget-handling errors in the baselines.
